LLM-based-system-one · Benchmark report · Jevals release

Local System One Agents vs Jev

This project implements an LLM-based system-one model based on open-weights models that answer like Jev, TypeSafe’s System One model: a typed yes/no, choice or score answer with a probability for every allowed answer. Each local model gives its probabilities two ways: read from its next-token log probabilities (readout) or written out as JSON (verbalized). The benchmark scores those probabilities on Jevals tasks and compares them with Jev and with a baseline that only knows how often each answer occurs.

Method

How to read the benchmark

Each task gives a system a question and a fixed set of allowed answers. The system picks one and puts a probability on every allowed answer. The benchmark scores the whole set of probabilities, not only the pick.

Where the probabilities come from

native
Jev returns a probability for every allowed answer. Taken from Jevals’ published logs.
verbalized
The model writes its probabilities out as JSON. One of the two ways LLM-based-system-one runs each local model.
readout
LLM-based-system-one reads the probabilities from the model’s next-token log probabilities. The model writes no text. Local models only.
base rates
The label prior: how often each answer occurs among the evaluated items. It never reads the question.

Color shows who produced the answers: blue for Jev, orange for LLM-based-system-one’s local models. An open orange dot is a local model with verbalized probabilities.

Decision Score, the headline metric

Decision Score = 100 × (1 − system loss ÷ label-prior loss)

0 means the same loss as the label prior and 100 means zero loss. Below 0 is worse than the label prior, with no lower bound. Loss is the mean Brier score on yes/no and choice tasks and the mean ranked probability score on score tasks, where a neighboring rating costs less than a distant one.

Accuracy
How often the most probable answer is the correct one.
ECE
Expected calibration error in percentage points: the gap between stated confidence and observed accuracy across 10 confidence bins. Lower is better.
Valid
Share of outputs in the required answer format. An invalid output is scored as an even spread over the allowed answers.
p50 / p95
Median and 95th-percentile time per decision, in milliseconds.
Decisions/s
Decisions divided by their summed decision time. Not throughput under concurrent load.

Fig. 5 · Other metrics

Accuracy and calibration can disagree with the score

Accuracy only counts whether the top answer is right, and ECE measures one property of the probabilities. The Decision Score rates the whole distribution, so a system can look good on one and poor on the other.

Interpretation limits

What these results do not show

  • Rescored, not rerunLLM-based-system-one did not call Jev. Jev’s rows rescore Jevals’ published answer logs with LLM-based-system-one’s scoring code. Decision Score, accuracy, loss, ECE, repeat and order flip rates and p50/p95 latency all matched the published board within rounding. That verifies the rescoring, not Jev’s original inference runs.
  • Own intervalsThe 95% intervals come from LLM-based-system-one’s item-cluster bootstrap. They are not board-checked and can differ from the published intervals.
  • Point estimates, not significanceRankings of point estimates do not establish significant differences. Overlapping intervals establish neither a difference nor equivalence.
  • No controlled speed test
  • Not measuredCost, energy use and privacy compliance. The benchmark establishes no advantage on any of them.
  • Combinations, not causesEach row is a complete model-and-method combination. The results describe these models and configurations and set no minimum model size for decision tasks.

Running locally gives control over where inference happens and which model is served, at the cost of managing the hardware and accepting the measured quality and latency. A deployment decision should still check the actual task’s probabilities, error consequences and latency requirements.
